Factor Regression and Hedge Research

Estimating market and sector betas for three NSE stocks, and checking whether a beta-sized Nifty futures position actually works as a hedge.

In plain terms

What this project is actually asking

If you own a stock and the whole market drops, your stock usually drops too, just because it's part of the market. A "hedge" is a separate bet, in the opposite direction, sized so that market-wide move gets cancelled out. The sizing number is called beta: how much the stock tends to move for every 1% the market moves. Each stock's single-factor beta below is measured against Nifty 50, the market itself.

This project asks two things. First: for three NSE stocks, how much of their daily movement actually comes from the broad market versus their own sector index? Second: if you built a hedge sized off that market beta, what would it actually buy you?

The answer to the second question turned out to be the more interesting one: a hedge like this cancels out both gains and losses in roughly equal proportion, not just the losses. That makes it a tool for managing risk, not a way to make money on its own, and the project ends up being as much about that distinction as about the regression itself.

Overview

CAPM betas, sector betas, and what each is actually good for

Three NSE stocks were regressed against Nifty 50 alone, then against Nifty 50 and their own sector index together, to see how much of each stock's return market and sector separately explain, and which one a Nifty futures hedge should actually be sized on.

The problem

Does a stock's daily return come mostly from the broad market, or mostly from its sector? And does a Nifty-sized hedge actually make sense given the answer?

What was built

A Fyers-based data pipeline, hand-rolled OLS (no statsmodels), and a full diagnostic suite: standard errors, t-stats, R², VIF, and condition number.

Universe

HDFCBANK, TATASTEEL, HINDUNILVR against Nifty 50, Nifty Bank, Nifty Metal, and Nifty FMCG.

Methodology

From raw prices to a hedge ratio

Python, using pandas, NumPy, and the Fyers API. OLS, standard errors, and diagnostics were all implemented from the normal equations rather than pulled from statsmodels.

01

Data source: NSE bhavcopy to Fyers

Data collection started on NSE bhavcopy archives, then moved to the Fyers API partway through. Betas on Fyers data matched the original bhavcopy betas closely, confirming the migration didn't introduce errors. Fyers auth runs on a token rather than IP, and its OHLCV history skips bhavcopy's index-naming quirks.

02

Corporate action handling

HDFCBANK's 1:1 bonus (ex-date 2025-08-26) gets auto-adjusted by Fyers. HINDUNILVR's demerger (ex-date 2025-12-05) isn't adjusted, so the ~-3.44% drop across that date stays in the data, since it reflects a real transfer of value out of the stock rather than a data error.

03

Single and two-factor OLS, from the normal equations

Each stock was regressed against Nifty 50 alone, then against Nifty 50 and its own sector index together. Beta, residuals, and fitted values all come from solving (XᵀX)β = XᵀY directly with NumPy, rather than a regression library.

04

Diagnostics: SE, t-stats, R², VIF, condition number

Standard errors come from σ²(XᵀX)⁻¹, t-stats from beta divided by SE, VIF from regressing each factor on the others and applying 1/(1−R²), and condition number from the eigenvalues of XᵀX. All five were built from scratch to check exactly how reliable each regression's coefficients were, not just what they were.

Two factors are correlated by construction: these stocks are large weights in their own sector indices
Results

Sector explains most of it, market mostly doesn't

Single-factor betas are clean estimates against Nifty 50 (market) alone, and are the right input for a Nifty hedge. The two-factor regression shows why: sector, not market, is doing the real work for two of the three stocks.

Single-factor (CAPM) betas against the market

Beta
How much the stock moves for every 1% the market moves. Above 1 means more volatile than the market, below 1 means less.
SE (standard error)
How much this beta would wobble if re-estimated on a different sample. Smaller is more precise.
t-stat
Beta divided by SE. Above roughly 2 in magnitude means the beta is statistically distinguishable from zero.
R²
Share of the stock's daily return variance explained by the market alone. Higher means the market matters more for this stock.

Two-factor regression: sector dominates for two of three stocks

β market / β sector
Each factor's coefficient, holding the other constant. Not directly comparable to the single-factor beta above, since each now reflects only the independent effect of that factor.
t market / t sector
Same idea as single-factor t-stats. Below ~2 in magnitude means that factor isn't adding statistically distinguishable explanatory power once the other factor is included.
R²
Share of variance explained by market and sector together.
VIF
How much a coefficient's uncertainty is inflated by overlap with the other regressor. Below 5 is usually fine, above 10 is a common concern threshold.
Condition number
A single measure of how unstable the regression's matrix math is overall. Above roughly 30 is a common concern threshold.

HDFCBANK and TATASTEEL's market coefficients both sit below |t|=2 once sector is in the model. Sector is carrying almost the entire regression, and the small negative market coefficient is what shows up when two correlated regressors compete for the same variance.

HINDUNILVR breaks that pattern. Its market coefficient stays significant (t = -5.50) even with sector included, meaning its low single-factor beta is really two effects netting out: strong positive co-movement with its sector, plus a separate, real pull against the broad market.

VIF and condition number disagree

Why they can disagree
VIF checks each regressor against the others one at a time. Condition number looks at the whole design matrix at once, including the constant term, so it can flag instability VIF misses.
Rule of thumb
VIF above 5–10, or condition number above roughly 30, are the usual thresholds worth treating as a caution flag.

VIF (4.42, 2.11, 1.48) stayed under the usual 5–10 concern range for all three stocks. Condition numbers told a different story, all sitting above 170 against a typical threshold of around 30. These two diagnostics measure related but distinct things.

Conclusions

A hedge is a risk tool, not a strategy

Sector drives HDFCBANK and TATASTEEL

Once sector is in the regression, market adds nothing statistically for these two stocks. A sector-instrument hedge (Bank Nifty, Nifty Metal) would likely outperform a Nifty hedge for them, though that wasn't built here, since it targets a different underlying instrument.

HINDUNILVR is a real exception

Its market sensitivity is real, not a multicollinearity artifact, and nets two offsetting effects rather than reflecting one weak relationship. Its hedge ratio is correct for Nifty, but the exposure underneath it is less simple than the number alone suggests.

A full hedge removes upside with downside

At R² of 0.58–0.80, a beta-sized hedge cuts a large share of both gains and losses. It has no edge built in, useful for isolating a stock-specific view or cutting volatility, but not for generating returns on its own.
