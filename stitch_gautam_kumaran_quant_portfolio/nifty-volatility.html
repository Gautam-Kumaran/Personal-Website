In plain terms

What this project is actually asking

This project examines a specific options strategy: selling Nifty options that are about 2% away from the current price on expiry day, betting they expire worthless. That strategy assumes a 2% move in a single day is unlikely.

In this project, that assumption is tested directly. It is not obvious how often Nifty actually moves 2% in a day, or whether that probability rises as expiry approaches, so the dashboard builds a data-backed answer using the full historical record instead of relying on the assumption.

The dashboard uses the Nifty 50's full daily trading history back to 2008 to test it directly. Users can set their own definition of a "big move," filter by direction, and see exactly how often that kind of move happens overall, and specifically in the days leading up to expiry. The result: big moves are not more frequent in expiry week than elsewhere on the calendar, but within expiry week they do skew more toward the downside.

Overview

Replacing a gut-feel assumption with the full trading history

Instead of eyeballing a few months of charts, the dashboard pulls every Nifty 50 trading day since 2008 and lets you slice it by move size, direction, year range, and distance from expiry, all filtered instantly in the browser.

The problem

The actual frequency of Nifty 50 moves of 2% or more in a day, and whether that frequency changes as monthly or weekly expiry approaches, is rarely quantified.

What was built

An offline data pipeline that downloads and tags every trading day, a Postgres-backed Flask API, and a vanilla JS dashboard that filters the full dataset client-side after a single API call.

Dataset

Daily OHLCV data for the Nifty 50 index, spanning 2008 to 2026, with every day tagged by return, gap, intraday move, and expiry timing.

Methodology

From raw prices to a filterable dataset

Python, using yfinance, pandas, pandas_market_calendars, and psycopg2, backed by PostgreSQL and served through Flask. The frontend is vanilla HTML, CSS, and JavaScript with no framework and no build step.

01

Data collection and derived stats

generate_data.py downloads Nifty 50 OHLCV history via yfinance and computes close-to-close return, overnight gap, intraday move, day range, and next-day return for every trading day since 2008.

02

Expiry calendar logic

NSE's monthly and weekly options expiry is not simply "the last Thursday of the month." It has to account for holidays shifting expiry to the previous trading day, weekly options not existing before February 2019, and SEBI's September 2025 rule change that moved the standard expiry day from Thursday to Tuesday. get_monthly_expiry() and get_weekly_expiry() walk backward from the theoretical expiry date to the nearest real NSE trading day, using an actual NSE holiday calendar rather than a fixed weekday assumption.

Regime tags: thursday / tuesday
03

Storage

load_to_postgres.py bulk-loads the generated data into a nifty50 Postgres table using execute_values for efficient batch inserts. The project started as a flat data.json file and was migrated to Postgres specifically to get hands-on practice with schema design and SQL.

04

API and frontend

app.py is a small Flask API that queries Postgres, groups results by year, and returns JSON shaped identically to the original data.json output. The frontend makes one call to /api/data on load, then all filtering (move size, direction, year range) happens client-side against that dataset, so the dashboard feels instant once it loads.

Results

Move frequency is proportional; direction is not

The numbers below come directly from the full 2008–2026 dataset.

2% moves are not rare

Across the full dataset, the Nifty 50 moved 2% or more in a single day on 8.3% of all trading days, 375 out of 4,530 days between 2008 and 2026.

Of those 375 big-move days, 17.1% happened overnight: the move was already priced in at the open, with no intraday warning. A strategy that only watches intraday price action would miss roughly one in six of these events entirely.

Once a threshold is crossed, most moves are moderate

Conditional on a day crossing the 2% threshold, the size of the move is not evenly spread. 60.8% land in the 2–3% band, 22.7% in 3–4%, 5.3% in 4–5%, and 11.2% exceed 5%.

The tail matters more than its share suggests: an 11.2% chance of a move beyond 5% is enough to blow through the credit collected on a 2%-OTM short strike, since the payoff of that trade is capped on the upside and effectively uncapped on the downside.

Big moves cluster: today's move raises tomorrow's odds

A big move is not an isolated event. Following one of the 375 big-move days, there is a 28.3% chance of another big move the very next day (106 of 375 cases), more than three times the unconditional 8.3% base rate.

Direction is roughly a coin flip on that follow-through day: 56.0% up versus 44.0% down. So clustering raises the odds of another big move without predicting which way it breaks, which matters for a strategy that assumes each expiry cycle is independent.

Expiry week's share of big moves is proportional, not elevated

Expiry week, the seven calendar days leading up to and including monthly expiry, makes up 27.8% of all trading days in the dataset. It accounts for 24.8% of big-move days. Big moves are not more frequent in expiry week than the rest of the calendar; if anything, the share is slightly lower.

Within expiry week, the direction skews more bearish

Across the full dataset, big moves split roughly 51.7% down / 48.3% up. Within expiry week specifically, that split shifts to 55.9% down / 44.1% up across 93 expiry-week big-move events, a modest additional lean toward the downside compared to the overall split.

What this data does and doesn't tell you

These numbers describe the risk side of the trade: how often big moves happen, how large they get once a threshold is crossed, how much warning you get, and how the odds shift near expiry. They don't say whether the premium collected is enough to compensate for that risk. That depends on the actual payoff structure of the trade, which isn't part of this analysis.

Conclusions

A measured baseline for expiry-week risk

Move frequency, not elevated near expiry

Expiry week accounts for 24.8% of big-move days while making up 27.8% of all trading days, so big moves are not disproportionately frequent near expiry.

Direction shifts within expiry week

The overall big-move split is close to even (51.7% down / 48.3% up). Within expiry week specifically, that shifts to 55.9% down / 44.1% up, a modest bearish lean.

What this means for the trade

The "sell 2%-OTM near expiry" assumption holds up on frequency: expiry week isn't riskier than any other week. But it isn't direction-neutral: the extra bearish lean in expiry week means put-side risk is understated more than call-side risk by a strategy that treats both directions as equally unlikely.
